Safeguarding

Worried about someone? Tell us.

Everyone who takes part in our work should feel safe, respected and listened to. You do not need to be certain that something is wrong before sharing a concern.

If there is immediate danger

Call 999.

If a child, young person or adult is in immediate danger or needs urgent medical help, contact the emergency services first.

Contact our safeguarding team

Email info@kingscentresouthall.org.uk with the subject “Safeguarding concern”. Your message will be passed promptly to the person responsible for safeguarding.

How to share a concern

  1. Contact the Centre promptly.
    Email info@kingscentresouthall.org.uk with the subject “Safeguarding concern”.
  2. Share what you know.
    Explain what happened, who is involved, when and where it happened, and any immediate risk. Do not investigate the concern yourself.
  3. We will listen and act.
    The concern should be passed to the designated safeguarding lead, recorded securely and handled under the Centre’s safeguarding procedures.

Privacy matters: do not include sensitive personal information in a general website form. We will explain the safest way to share any further details.

Our responsibility

Safeguarding belongs in every programme.

  • Clear expectations for staff, volunteers and participants.
  • Appropriate checks, training and supervision.
  • Simple reporting routes that young people and adults can understand.
  • Secure records and careful information sharing.
  • Regular review of policies and practice.
